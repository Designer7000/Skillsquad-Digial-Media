Case Study — Packaging · 2024

Verde Organics

Shelf-dominating packaging for an organic range — oversized type, deep green fields and the confidence to be the loudest thing in the aisle.

01

Overview

Verde makes organic pantry staples with honest sourcing and no shortcuts — and was losing the shelf to brands with weaker products and louder packs. Skillsquad redesigned the full range: pack structure, illustration, typography and a back-panel system that treats the ingredient list as a feature, not a legal requirement.

The engagement covered eight SKUs, dielines and print production, shelf renders for buyer meetings, and art direction for the range's retail debut. The brief was blunt: make the product findable from ten feet and delightful from ten inches.

02

The Challenge

The organic aisle is a paradox: everything claims to be natural, so nothing looks natural. Verde's old packs used the same muted kraft-and-leaf language as every competitor — and read as own-label next to the confident national brands.

The range also had to survive two distances. From ten feet, a shopper must spot the brand; from ten inches, a sceptic must trust it. Most packaging solves one distance and fails the other. Verde needed both, on a timeline set by a retail listing window.

03

Strategy

Strategy: botanical brutalism. Instead of whispering "natural" like the rest of the aisle, Verde would own the shelf with oversized type on deep green fields — one confident produce illustration per SKU, nothing decorative, nothing apologetic. Where competitors used thirty words, Verde used three.

Variants were colour-blocked so the range reads as a family from across the store, and the back panel was redesigned around radical honesty: big type for what matters, the full ingredient story for the sceptics, and sourcing claims kept short enough to be believed.

On a crowded shelf, confidence is the loudest colour.
04

Creative Direction

Direction: the produce crate, modernised. Matte stock with a tactile finish, embossed marks you find with your fingers, and typography set like signage rather than labels. References came from vintage seed packets, produce crates and botanical plates — honest graphics from before marketing existed.

Each SKU carries a single hand-drawn produce illustration, scaled large and cropped tight, so the range feels like a harvest rather than a product line. The emboss catches the aisle light; the matte keeps it grounded. Premium you feel before you read.

05

Design System

  • Ink#0A0A0B
  • Paper#F4F3EF
  • Amber — seals only#FDB815
  • Leaf — project tint#6FA356
Aa.
  • Display — Space Grotesk Bold. Oversized, tight tracking, signage-scale. The product name is the headline.
  • Body — Inter. For back panels and legal — honest information set with the same care as the front.
  • Three-word rule — the front of pack makes its point in three words or fewer.
  • Dieline gridA shared structural grid across all eight SKUs — one system, scaled to every pack size.
  • Variant colour-blockingEach variant owns a green depth; the range reads as a family from across the aisle.
  • Back-panel hierarchyWhat matters in big type, the full story below it, claims short enough to be believed.
  • Print specificationMatte laminate with embossed marks — premium felt in the hand, not just seen.
06

Final Execution

The range shipped print-ready with dielines, shelf renders for buyer presentations, and a retailer deck that sold the story before the product hit the pallet. Art direction extended to launch photography so the digital shelf matched the physical one.

07

Results

Results are measured against client KPIs and published with permission. For Verde, that means retail sell-through, listing expansion and unaided shelf recognition — tracked from the range relaunch.

The packaging was designed to win twice: findable from ten feet, trustworthy from ten inches — the two distances that decide whether a pack leaves the shelf.
